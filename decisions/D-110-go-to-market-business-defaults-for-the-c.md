## Context

The console v2 audit (music-jetkreate-web/planning/ui-ux-audit/11) left 8 business questions open. We launch with a small team, a monolith on 1-2 instances, Google models only, and as little paid infrastructure as possible. D-100 already set credits and the ledger as the money model, with store purchase in the app and Razorpay on the web 'later'. D-089 keeps PostHog for analytics and flags. D-109 shipped the console backend on the proposals. This record answers each question the way an early product with a small budget normally would, so the web console can be built against fixed answers. It confirms D-109 and does not replace it.

## Options considered

Q1: subscriptions, credit packs, or both; pay at launch or later. Q2: recognise revenue on purchase or on use. Q3: integrated billing exports or hand entry; INR or USD. Q4: the proposed roles or fewer; build MFA or use Google's. Q5: retention length; purge or anonymise. Q6: PostHog-based or AI-call-based activity; churn window. Q7: impersonation, a read-only token, or neither. Q8: hosted monitoring (Datadog, Grafana Cloud) or what the app already has.

## Decision

Q1 MONEY. Phase 0, now until launch plus about 6 weeks: free credits only, as in D-100. No payment code, so we learn the real cost per render and repeat use first. Phase 1: credit packs only, no subscriptions. Packs are one-time purchases, so there are no renewals, proration, failed-payment chasing or cancellation flows, and each pack maps to one 'purchase' ledger entry that already exists. Start with 3 packs. Price each so credits sell for at least 2.5x our measured AI cost after the store's cut. That uses /billing/unit-costs, a 15% store fee (join the Apple Small Business Program and Google's 15% tier) and tax. In the app: Apple and Google in-app purchase through RevenueCat, free under USD 2.5k a month in store revenue. On the web: Razorpay, but only in Phase 2 and only if web users ask to pay. The currency is INR, with each store's price tiers handling other countries; no USD price list of our own. Purchased credits expire 12 months after purchase, stated at checkout. Free and signup credits follow the plan's own rollover rules. Phase 2: subscriptions, only if Phase 1 data shows people buying repeatedly. A subscription is a plan with a monthly refill, which plans already support. Q2 REVENUE. Recognise revenue as credits are used: unused paid credits are a liability. Recognise expired paid credits as breakage in proportion to use. This is the usual treatment under Ind AS 115 / IFRS 15, and /billing/liability already measures both. The console shows 'collected' (cash, gross, store fee, tax, net) and 'recognised' side by side. The accountant confirms the rule before the first sale. Purchase records are kept for 8 years (Indian books of account): a deleted user's purchase and ledger rows stay with the user id but no profile data (the ledger is already kept on deletion). Q3 FINANCE INPUTS. A person with the finance role, a founder until we hire one, enters last month's cost lines and month-end cash by the 7th of each month, from the invoices (GCP, Mongo Atlas, PostHog, domains and tools, people, marketing). AI provider invoices are entered as ai-provider:<name>, so the console shows invoice against measured. The console shows the inputs' last-published date, and turns it red after the 10th if last month is missing. Currency of record in the console is USD, because cloud and AI invoices are in USD and measured cost is in USD. INR bills are entered at that month's RBI reference rate, stored in fx. The statutory books stay in INR in the accounting software. No billing-export or accounting integration until there is revenue. Q4 ROLES. Keep D-109's five roles. Owners are at most 2 founders. Everyone has their own account; no shared logins. Support's cap stays 500 credits per grant; more goes to finance. MFA: not built. Instead, admin roles are given only to company Google Workspace accounts that sign in with Google, with 2-step verification enforced in the Workspace admin console. That gives MFA at no cost. Backend follow-up when ready: refuse admin access to a non-Workspace or password sign-in (a small change, needs its own request). Build TOTP only if a non-Workspace admin is ever needed. Q5 RETENTION. 400 days for the admin audit log (built). On account deletion, anonymise rather than purge (built): the trail of who did what is a security record that DPDP lets us keep, and after redaction it holds ids, not personal data. Revisit if legal advice says otherwise. Q6 DEFINITIONS. Active user (product analytics: DAU/WAU/MAU) = a signed-in person who produced any event in the period (the current queries). Active creator (unit economics, cost per user) = at least one AI call in the period (built). North-star metric = weekly creators with at least one completed render. Churn = no activity for 28 days after at least one completed render (built). Once payments exist, a lapsed payer = no purchase within 90 days of their last pack. Activation = completed render within 7 days of signup (from the activation_funnel and time_to_first_render queries). Q7 VIEW-AS: no. No impersonation and no read-only token. Support uses user detail, events, renders and the export. Revisit only if support regularly cannot answer a ticket from those. Q8 DEPLOYMENT AND MONITORING. One Docker container, monolith on 1-2 instances. No paid monitoring. Use what is built: /api/admin/ops (Mongo per-minute rollup, provider history from GenerationLog, loops), server_alert events in PostHog plus ALERT_WEBHOOK_URL to the team chat, and /api/health watched every 1-5 minutes by a free external uptime check (Google Cloud uptime check if on GCP, otherwise a free uptime service). Revisit at more than 2 instances. ASSUMPTIONS 1-8 in doc 11: all accepted as written. For 7, the web session picks the chart library with its own decision record; prefer one small library over hand-rolled SVG once the console has more than a few chart types. CHECKED ON 2026-11-26, previously unverified: (a) ui-config.killSwitch: the app falls back to its built-in config defaults (music-jetkreate-mobile/src/lib/config/index.ts). It is an escape hatch for bad config, not a maintenance mode. Maintenance = the renders-enabled kill switch, plus the announcements bundle if JET-457's option is approved. (b) Verification OTP emails are NOT delivered: the backend stores the code but has no email sender (only Firebase sends the password-reset email). So the 'grant only to verified email' switch stays off (JET-385). Next step is a backend request to send verification through Firebase Auth's own email, which is free and already a dependency. (c) billing:null on user detail was fixed in JET-458. (d) Mobile client events (JET-373) are still waiting, so the platform and app-version segments wait too. (e) The 390px layout is for the web session to check.

## Reason

Each answer picks what needs no new infrastructure, no new spend before revenue, and no new code where the existing build already covers it. Credit packs before subscriptions is the usual order for usage-priced AI products: it matches the credit ledger we already have, and it avoids the recurring-billing work that subscriptions bring on both stores. Workspace 2-step verification gives MFA without building it. USD for operating numbers matches where the costs are, while the statutory books stay in INR.

## Consequences

Nothing in the backend changes now; D-109's defaults are confirmed. Follow-up requests when the time comes: (1) Phase 1 payments: RevenueCat webhook, Payment model, packs, a mobile purchase screen (JET-454 has the estimate); (2) admin access only through Workspace Google sign-in; (3) verification email through Firebase; (4) a free uptime check on /api/health. Web console: show 'collected' vs 'recognised' revenue; make finance inputs' freshness obvious; use these definitions on every label (active user, active creator, churn, activation).
